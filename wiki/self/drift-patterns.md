# Drift patterns

How Andrew's system fails under load — in his own words, "not hypothetical … observed." The point is predictive
accuracy, not self-criticism [LP §Addendum 1].

## Primary drift: upward into abstraction
Under load he moves **up into abstraction instead of down into embodiment**: thinking instead of moving, planning
instead of finishing, refining frameworks instead of doing the smallest viable action. "This drift feels productive,
intelligent, and justified — which makes it dangerous."

**Common manifestations:** generating plans faster than closing loops; substituting strategic thinking for physical
care; delaying health behaviours as "non-urgent"; overestimating next week's energy.

**Rationalisations:** "This thinking will save time later"; "Once I clarify this, everything else will follow"; "I'll
reset after this busy patch"; "I just need one clean run at it."

## Second drift: generosity as avoidance
Under emotional or cognitive pressure: spending money to relieve internal tension; saying yes to requests that reduce
friction but add load; being generous to others at the expense of his own structure. It "feels relationally positive,
but often erodes long-term stability."

## High-risk conditions
Several nights of poor sleep; work politically blocked or ambiguous; feeling he isn't doing enough for Elliott;
unresolved financial anxiety. "Under these conditions, clarity alone is not enough. I require containment."
[LP §Addendum 1]

## Confirmed since
In August 2026 Andrew named the same pattern unprompted — sophisticated articulation outrunning shipped work — while
planning a five-week sprint [conversation 2026-08-10 — The Sprint]; and it was the central risk identified when the
Choices document was read against the plan [conversation 2026-08-11 — Life Plan]. The redundancy period hits two
high-risk conditions at once (financial anxiety, ambiguous work), so [[stabilisation-protocol]] applies more than usual.
