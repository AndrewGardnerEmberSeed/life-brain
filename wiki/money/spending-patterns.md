# Spending patterns

What the statements show about how money leaks, from three analyses: 2025, February 2026 and fourteen months to July
2026. The Life Plan's diagnosis: the risk is "not frivolity — it is generosity without containment" [LP §Financial
Strategy 5.3].

## Known overspend channels
On Elliott (toys, experiences), on partner experiences (dining, outings), on team generosity (coffee, lunches)
[LP §Profile 7]; and convenience spending that "buys down internal pressure while weakening structure"
[LP §Five-Year Plan 2026].

## 2025: why the $20,000 savings goal was missed
About $15–18k a year in convenience spending — "living promotion to promotion." The cuts identified: daily bought
lunch (~$4,570 a year), daily coffee (~$3,000), credit-card gambling triggering cash-advance fees (~$1,200), impulse
retail at Kmart, Amazon, Bunnings, Jaycar, JB Hi-Fi (~$5,000), and tech subscriptions (~$1,500) — about $15,270 in all.
Rent ($1,020) and child support ($800) were consistent and well handled [LP §2025 evaluation].

## February 2026
Net salary $8,470 in two pays; net surplus **$22**. Credit-card spend $4,869, including about $825 on web and business
services (domain hosting, Squarespace, ASIC), large single supermarket shops alongside daily bought lunches and fast
food, and a Western Australia trip. A $3,000 card repayment on payday briefly overdrew the account by $2,610; a $1
online bet cost a $3.50 cash-advance fee. **Directives for March:** unlink the card from betting sites; a 24-hour gap
between salary clearing and large transfers; cap supermarket spend and enforce meal prep; clear the remaining $3,617
card balance [LP §February 2026 Financial Diagnostic].

## Fourteen months to July 2026
The card was never cleared in full (balance drifting from $1.8k to $4.3k, ~$107 a month in interest); the transaction
account closed between $390 and $1,508 for twelve straight months; vehicle costs spiky (rego ~$724 twice a year,
services $330–975); groceries and dining both high, meaning groceries weren't being cooked; **child support never
missed** [financial_tracker.html, Trends]. Monthly card spend ran $5,323–$8,753, with peaks in August, November,
December and May.

## Status
The January review called finance "a measurement gap, not yet a discipline failure" [LP §January review]; February
closed the gap and found the leaks; August built a budget around them ([[budget-and-runway]]). Whether the card was
cleared and the betting link removed: **Q08**.
